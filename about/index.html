How it works

From the muscles you pick to every rep you lift.

  1. Step 1

    Pick your muscles

    Tap the muscle groups you want to train. Then pick days a week, your experience and your equipment.

    Sample data: chest and back are picked, and further down, quads and glutes.

  2. Step 2

    Get a weekly plan

    fitee builds your week: sets per muscle for your level, big lifts first. Rather plan your own? Log your own workouts and save the routines you repeat.

    Shown with sample data.

  3. Step 3

    Prop up your phone

    Side-on for most lifts, front-on for some. A setup check makes sure the camera can see what the lift needs, from the right angle. Then recording starts and stops by itself. Or use a video you already have.

    Shown with sample data.

  4. Step 4

    See every rep

    fitee counts your reps and checks each one, with a skeleton drawn over your video. When the camera can’t see well enough, it says “couldn’t judge” instead of guessing. Each fault comes with a cue and an exercise that helps.

    • Passed
    • Fault
    • ?Couldn’t judge

    Sample data: the app’s demo figure stands in for a lifter. Rep 3 stops short of depth; rep 4’s depth couldn’t be judged.

  5. Step 5

    The coach adjusts

    Simple rules, on your phone. It adds weight once every set reaches the top of your rep range. On the {{FULL_COUNT}} lifts with full analysis, it also waits until your latest check on that lift was clean. The same fault in two of your last three checks? A lighter load, plus an exercise that helps. Lifts stalling? It offers an easier week.

    Sample data: depth missed in two of the last three squat checks.

{{FULL_COUNT}} lifts with full analysis
Back squat, bench press, deadlift, overhead press, barbell row and Romanian deadlift. The checks follow written coaching and competition standards (NSCA, IPF rules) and published research, with our own starting numbers where those give none. They haven’t been tested against real, labelled reps yet, so treat them as a guide.
{{EXERCISE_COUNT}} exercises in the library
Free weights, bodyweight, cables and machines, each with an animated how-to demo. The rest get basic analysis: camera placement, plus rep counting and, for most, range of motion. Holds such as the plank get a check on the position instead.

Your log

Every set, in one place.

Every workout lands in your log with its sets, reps and weights, whether the plan built it or you did.

  • A calendar of every workout
  • Progress charts and records for every exercise
  • A weekly body map, shaded by the sets each muscle got
03

On your phone

No signal down in the basement? fitee doesn’t need one.

Nothing to upload: fitee does its work on your phone, so your training stays there too.

  • Works with no signal

    Installed on a phone’s home screen, fitee saves its {{POSE_MB}} pose model the first time it’s opened online, if it’s left open until that’s done. After that it needs no signal.

  • Video never leaves your phone

    The pose model runs on the phone itself. No uploads, no analytics.

  • Video kept only if you save it

    Tap Save video after a check to keep the video, on your phone only. Otherwise the video is gone when you close the results. The set’s numbers and skeleton replay stay in your log, on your phone.

  • Your log stays on your phone

    So do your plans and profile. Backups are files you keep.

The vision

A plan shaped by how you lift.

That’s where fitee is going: a phone app that analyses your lifting and shapes your whole training plan around the technique it sees. Like a food diary, but for lifting.

It’s early: fitee is a first prototype, in testing now.
